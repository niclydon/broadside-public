# Broadside Editorial Playbook

Broadside is for moving real work into publishable writing quickly. Drafts should
surface the timely reason to care, make one clear promise, and preserve the
specificity that makes the writer's work credible.

## Headline And Dek Patterns

Use these patterns when generating, reviewing, or revising Substack titles,
LinkedIn first lines, campaign hooks, and newsletter subject/subheader pairs.

| Pattern | Title shape | Dek shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Why-now | `Why ___ Is Spiking Right Now` | A timely trigger, what changed, and who is affected. Urgent without hype. |
| Counterintuitive | `The Best Way to ___? Do Less.` | The surprising angle and the narrow condition where it is true. Clarity, not shock. |
| Data hook | `We Analyzed ___; Here's the Outlier` | One standout stat, why it matters, and one implication. Do not drown the reader in numbers. |
| First-person reporter | `I Tried ___ for 14 Days` | What was expected, what actually happened, and one lesson readers can use today. |
| Playful contrast | `Boardroom Rules, Playground Tools` | Juxtapose two worlds to show a fresh method. Clever is fine; cutesy is not. |
| How-to / benefit | `How to ___ in 30 Minutes` | Outcome, minimal steps, and a guardrail against a common pitfall. Concrete beats clever. |
| Short and bold | `Stop Measuring Vanity` | One declarative takeaway, one sharp reason, and a next action. |

## Fill-In Templates

- Why-now: `Why ____ Is Breaking This Quarter` — `A new ____ is pushing ____ to rethink ____ right as ____ bites.`
- Counterintuitive: `To Grow ____ , Cut ____` — `We found subtracting ____ beats adding ____ when ____ is constrained.`
- Data hook: `Only 7% of ____ Do This` — `It triples ____; here's how to copy it without adding headcount.`
- First-person: `I Replaced ____ With ____` — `What I feared, what surprised me, and the workflow I'm keeping.`
- Playful contrast: `Wall Street Focus, Montessori Teams` — `A childlike constraint made our sprints calmer and faster.`
- How-to / benefit: `Ship a Trustworthy AI Update Today` — `Scope, test, and message in three moves, no new tooling required.`
- Short and bold: `Delete Half Your Metrics` — `You'll get faster decisions and fewer heroics by noon.`

## Craft Rules

- Use strong nouns and verbs. Prune adjectives.
- Make one promise per headline. Let the dek carry nuance.
- For email, front-load the benefit in the first 6-8 words.
- Avoid clickbait words such as `shocking` and `insane`; use precise verbs such as `triples`, `cuts`, or `prevents`.
- For LinkedIn, aim for a 65-80 character headline or first-line hook. The dek becomes the first line or first short paragraph.
- For Substack, use the dek as the subheader and the first sentence of the email body when it fits naturally.
- Keep credibility anchored in specifics: numbers, dates, model names, constraints, tradeoffs, failure modes, and concrete reader actions.

## Agent Guidance

When asked to improve or prepare a draft for publish, return 5-7 headline/dek
pairs across several patterns rather than one "best" option. Label the pattern,
keep options skimmable, and prefer credible specificity over dramatic phrasing.
